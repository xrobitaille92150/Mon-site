SimCorp Dimension & Clearwater Analytics

I have delivered full implementations on both platforms, from scoping to production. Few independent consultants have taken SimCorp Dimension and Clearwater Analytics all the way through. The latest: Clearwater Analytics at a French reinsurer, live since January 2026.

The problem

An investment platform implementation is, first of all, an accounting project: chart of accounts mapping, multi-GAAP accounting schemes (local GAAP, IFRS 9), the data feed for Solvency II reporting, custodian reconciliation, suspense account clearing and the cut-over of interfaces to the general ledger.

The vendor knows its platform; the client knows its portfolios. Between the two sit the decisions that determine the outcome: how to migrate historical data, which flows to build with Bloomberg, Swift and the general ledger, and which operating model to set between front, middle and back office and accounting. Those decisions belong to whoever understands both sides.

A second item weighs on the timeline: data. Securities, reference data and amortised cost histories have to be rebuilt in the new platform, at the level of quality the new platform requires. In my experience, this is the workstream that slips most often.

What I deliver

  • Scoping and specification: functional requirements, target flows, migration strategy, requirements document, roadmap.
  • Accounting and controls: accounting schemes by GAAP and by asset class, amortised cost and impairment rules, closing controls, front / back / custodian reconciliation. See Investment Accounting & Reporting.
  • Testing and migration: design and execution of UAT campaigns, migration of reference data and histories, suspense account clearing, parallel runs.
  • Go-live and hypercare: cut-over coordination, production stabilisation, user documentation and training.
  • Governance: steering and sponsor committees, RAID log, deliverables in French and English. See PMO & Programme Delivery.
  • Both sides of the table: engaged by the vendor to secure its delivery, or by the institution to hold the timeline with the vendor. Never both on the same project.

The four workstreams that set the timeline

Whatever the platform, the same four workstreams decide whether the go-live date holds. I take them on from day one.

Reference data and history take-on. Securities master, counterparties, portfolios, then the amortised cost histories and open positions at cut-over date. The take-on is rehearsed several times on live data before the final run, with a reconciliation to the general ledger at each rehearsal.

Accounting schemes by GAAP and by asset class. Local GAAP, IFRS 9 and, where relevant, US GAAP, written entry by entry before configuration starts, then checked against the entries the platform produces. The Solvency II asset reporting is fed from the same data.

Reconciliation and suspense clearing. Front office, accounting and custodian compared line by line; suspense items assigned an owner and a clearing date. On the Clearwater implementation, custodian reconciliation moved from weekly to daily and the closing was cut from D+4 to D+2.

UAT and parallel run. Test cases written from real transactions, business teams staffed for the campaign, and at least one full closing produced on both systems before the legacy one is switched off.

Questions I am asked before an engagement

How long does a SimCorp Dimension or Clearwater Analytics implementation take at an insurer?

In my experience, 12 to 24 months for a full scope, from order management to accounting and regulatory reporting. The duration depends less on the platform than on the number of GAAPs to produce, the quality of the data to migrate and the availability of business teams for UAT. The Clearwater implementation at the reinsurer took 18 months for three modules.

Do you work for the vendor or for the institution?

Both, on different projects. For a vendor, I am a programme manager who knows the insurer's constraints from the inside: closing calendar, Solvency II deadlines, auditors, custodian. Decisions on the client side get taken faster. For an institution, I am the business owner who knows the platform and investment accounting, and who can hold a technical discussion with the vendor's teams as a peer. Never both roles on the same project.

Is a migration from Linedata Chorus a special case?

Yes. I have led two complete replacements of Linedata Chorus, one to SimCorp Dimension and one to Clearwater Analytics. The hard points are similar: securities reference data take-on, rebuilding amortised cost histories, positions and suspense items at cut-over date, and a parallel run over at least one closing.

Which accounting and regulatory frameworks do you cover on these platforms?

French GAAP (ANC regulation 2015-11), IFRS 9 (amortised cost, expected credit losses, classification), US GAAP, and the Solvency II asset reporting (QRT S.06.02 among others). I configure the accounting schemes and check the entries produced, line by line, before go-live.
